# Five-minute test — H1 v2

Read EXPERIMENT.md first. No sessions have happened yet. This protocol was revised before testing.

## Recruit
Find up to 3 students coordinating a real group assignment due within seven days, with at least one task without an agreed owner and access to their team's chat/shared document. Record invitations, eligible starts, and consent separately. Do not count developer checks, the owner, or sample-data runs.

## Consent
“This is a student prototype. Participation is voluntary, you can stop anytime, and you do not have to share a plan. Use aliases and avoid confidential assignment information. May I record anonymous actions and feedback for class?”

## Session
1. Open a fresh blank planner and start the clock.
2. Say: “Make a plan for your current assignment. When you're finished, do what you normally would with a plan you want your team to use.”
3. Observe silently. Do not coach the controls or suggest sharing. Help means unassisted success is false.
4. Record generation, review/edit, export, and actual voluntary placement in the team's existing chat/document separately. Never capture the message or recipient. Let the participant control any sharing. If ethical observation is not possible, mark it unverified.
5. At five minutes, stop measuring success. You can continue discussion afterward.
6. Ask: “What would you otherwise have done? Where did this get in your way? What would your team change?”
7. Optional exploratory question, after H1 is complete: “Would a human review of this plan be useful enough to pay $5 per team? Why?” This is an opinion, not payment evidence and not H1 success.

## Send the builder anonymous facts
Invitations: N
P1 / eligible / consent / started / time / own assignment / generated / reviewed / copied or downloaded / actual team-workflow placement observed / help needed / friction / feedback

Use yes/no/unverified. Do not send names, message contents, or sensitive course details. Preserve failed and incomplete sessions.

## Revision
Prioritize a problem that blocked adoption. Describe the exact change, then repeat the affected task with the participant and record whether it works. A repeated tester is a within-person verification, not an independent new customer or part of the original denominator. Keep both attempts. If you cannot retest, say so.
