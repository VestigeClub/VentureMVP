# H1 — A real team uses the planning output

## Precommitment history

Version 2, written on 2026-09-08 after reviewing the complete rubric and before any participant testing. Version 1 proposed export plus stated intended use; it was never run. Version 2 replaces stated intention with an observable action in the student's existing team workflow. No results informed this change. A pre-publication clarification explicitly included coordination responsibility and defined observable review. No participant sessions had occurred. Freeze these criteria before the first session.

## Specific customer, costly job, and buying moment

Customer: the student informally coordinating a college group assignment due within the next seven days, where at least one task still lacks an agreed owner.
Current alternative: group-chat messages plus a manually assembled list or shared document.
Unvalidated pain: the coordinator repeatedly follows up, some work gets duplicated or missed, and unfinished work becomes a last-minute burden. No time-saving estimate or grade improvement has been measured.
Buying moment: before the team starts or divides the remaining work, when the deadline makes another coordination round costly.
Proposed payer: the coordinating student buying a $5 human plan review for the team. This is a separate, unvalidated offer and not currently sold.

## Consequential hypothesis

At least 2 of the first 3 eligible, consenting students who begin will independently build and review a plan for their real assignment and voluntarily place it into the team's existing group chat or shared planning document within five minutes. Merely copying, downloading, liking, or saying they would use it does not meet the rule.

Why it matters: a usable artifact must replace part of the existing coordination workflow before a paid review merits further development. This tests initial workflow adoption, not team agreement, repeat use, time saved, willingness to pay, or market demand.

## Frozen protocol and decision rule

- Planned denominator: first 3 eligible, consenting starts. Count failures and dropouts. Record total invitations and ineligible/declined separately.
- Eligibility: participant currently coordinates the team’s work; current group assignment due within seven days; participant has access to the team's existing chat/document; at least one task has no agreed owner.
- Start clock when the participant first sees the blank planner.
- Task: “Make a plan for your current assignment. When you're finished, do what you normally would with a plan you want your team to use.” Do not instruct the participant to share or coach the controls.
- Review observation: count an unprompted owner/date adjustment, or an explicit unprompted acceptance after the participant checks the assignments. Silent viewing alone is unverified. Do not require an edit or prompt acceptance during the timed task.
- Record whether participants belong to the same team. Multiple students from one team are not independent team adoptions.
- Success requires all within five minutes without help: own assignment entered; plan generated; owners/dates reviewed; actual placement into existing team workflow voluntarily observed.
- Copy/download is an intermediate event, recorded separately. Saying “I shared it” without observation is self-report, not an observed success.
- No obligation to share. Opting not to share is acceptable and recorded as no adoption.
- If at least 2 of 3 succeed: continue to a larger actual-team-use test and a separately precommitted paid concierge test. Do not claim product-market fit.
- If 0 or 1 of 3 succeed: change the most consequential observed friction, then test the revision. Do not build the paid service yet.
- If the revision still has 0 or 1 of 3 successes in a new comparable sample: stop this version of the solution and revisit the problem/workflow.
- If fewer than 3 start: incomplete; report raw counts and observed friction. No pass/fail claim.

## Ethics and privacy

Read: “This is a student prototype. Participation is voluntary, you can stop anytime, and you do not have to share a plan. Use aliases and avoid confidential assignment information. May I record anonymous actions and feedback for class?”

Observe only the minimum action needed with participant permission. Do not record messages, names, contacts, the class's materials, screens, or recipients. The participant controls any sharing. If sharing cannot be ethically observed, mark it unverified and do not count it as success. Do not send material on the participant's behalf.

No accounts, analytics, payment collection, or server storage in the planner. Inputs remain in the browser tab; export creates a local file and clipboard action copies the plan. Raw anonymous notes: delete after grading/appeals and no later than 30 days after final grading. Keep aggregate counts only if needed.
