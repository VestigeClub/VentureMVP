# Fairshare: 90-second pitch rehearsal

Use the latest pitch.pptx in this folder (Fairshare-Pitch-1-Final.pptx in the submission outputs). This is ready for practice. After the real practice attempt, revise the actual claim based on advice, then submit the slides you actually use for the graded re-pitch. The public product URL is https://VestigeClub.github.io/VentureMVP/. Confirm the site is available on the presentation machine before starting.

## Before you start

Open the deck and the planner in separate windows. Load the sample inputs before the clock starts. Close unrelated tabs and notifications. Learn the story; do not read the script word for word. Rehearse with a stopwatch and aim to finish by 85 seconds so one slow click does not push you over 90.

## 0–25 seconds: slide 1

“The deadline is tomorrow. Your group chat is busy, but nobody owns the slides. The student coordinating the project ends up chasing teammates or doing the missing work. Fairshare gives that coordinator a starting plan with an owner and date for every task.”

## 25–60 seconds: slide 2, then live product

“Enter tasks, estimated hours, teammates, and a deadline. Fairshare distributes work using estimated hours. Here, I generate the plan, change an owner, watch workload totals update, and copy it into the team’s existing chat. The team must check availability and whether the dates make sense.”

Spend about 15 seconds showing the actual product:

1. Switch to the sample inputs and click Build my plan to show the allocation step.
2. Change Research customer needs from Sam to Alex. Totals change from 3/3/3 to 5/1/3 hours.
3. Click Copy for group chat. Point to the success message. Do not send the sample to a real group.
4. Return to slide 3 by 60 seconds.

## 60–90 seconds: slide 3

“The planner is free. I propose a five-dollar human review, paid by the coordinator, to check the task list against the assignment brief, flag missing work, and suggest assignments based on availability. That service is not available yet, and payment demand is untested. First, I am testing whether students voluntarily use the plan in their real group workflow.”

## Practice feedback to collect

Ask the pod what they understood: Who is the customer? What makes the problem costly? Who pays, and for what? Which claim was unclear or unconvincing? Record one actual piece of advice and the specific claim you change. Use pitch-revision.md in the source package.

## If the live demo fails

Do not imply the table is live. Say “This is the sample output; the live demo is unavailable right now.” Use slide 2 to explain the mechanism and finish on time. This fallback does not satisfy the live-demo rubric as strongly, so test the public site and your presentation machine beforehand.

## Be able to explain

- The algorithm assigns the largest estimated task to the least-loaded teammate, then repeats. It does not use AI at runtime.
- Balanced hours do not guarantee equal difficulty, capacity, or skills. Users must review the draft.
- Dates follow task order and reserve the final day for review when possible. The planner cannot certify feasibility.
- The current product is free. The $5 human review is a hypothesis and is not offered yet.
- H1 tests voluntary placement in a real team workflow. A copied plan or positive opinion alone is not success.
- AI helped create code and documents. You own the experiment, observed evidence, economic judgment, and final presentation.
